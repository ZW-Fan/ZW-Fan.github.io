About

I want neural networks that understand the physics they model. A network that respects conservation laws, symmetries and the right physical observables makes predictions that can be checked and trusted, and becomes a working tool for engineering and device design rather than a black-box fit. My work builds such models for nonlinear systems governed by partial differential equations.

The path so far

I trained as an electronic engineer and moved into computational physics during my MSc at Tel Aviv University with Boris Malomed, simulating Gross–Pitaevskii dynamics of ultracold atomic gases — solitons and nonlinear matter waves in Bose–Einstein condensates.

For my PhD at the University of Bath, as a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow with Dmitry Skryabin, I turned to computational photonics — Kerr microresonators, dissipative solitons and optical frequency combs. Within the EU training network, my secondments and collaborations spanned academic laboratories and photonics companies — EPFL, IBM Research Zurich, the University of Freiburg, Ghent University, Yale University, Luceda Photonics and Menlo Systems — where simulations had to answer to experiments and device constraints.

Since 2024 I have worked at Newcastle University, where the two threads meet machine learning — physics-consistent neural operators for nonlinear wave dynamics, and Koopman / DMD methods that read spectral structure directly from data.

The approach has stayed the same throughout — start from the physics, let numerical evidence decide, and add model complexity only when the evidence asks for it.
