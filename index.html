Computational physicist · Scientific machine learning

I want neural networks that understand the physics they model. A network that respects conservation laws, symmetries and the right physical observables makes predictions that can be checked and trusted, and becomes a working tool for engineering and device design rather than a black-box fit. My work builds such models for nonlinear systems governed by partial differential equations.

Experience

My work follows one line — from quantum gases, through computational photonics and photonic device design, to machine learning for physics. The equations have stayed nonlinear throughout; what has changed is how much of the physics can be learned from data, and how directly it can be built into the model.

  1. Quantum gasesMatter-wave solitons in Bose–Einstein condensates
  2. Computational photonicsKerr microresonators, dissipative solitons and frequency combs
  3. Photonic designDevice-oriented modelling with photonics labs and companies
  4. Data-driven physicsPhysics-consistent machine learning and data-driven discovery
