Nonlinear waves in light and matter

The same nonlinear wave equations describe light circulating in a microresonator and atoms in a Bose–Einstein condensate. In optics I work on Kerr and χ⁽²⁾ microresonators — dissipative solitons, frequency combs, symmetry breaking and soliton crystals. In cold atoms, earlier work with Boris Malomed and collaborators studied gap and embedded solitons, and two-dimensional solitons in dipolar and spin–orbit-coupled condensates.

Highlight · Topological soliton metacrystals

A periodic sequence of bound soliton pairs rotating in a ring microresonator acts as an effective metamaterial in the radio-to-terahertz range. The separation of the two solitons within each unit cell is the control parameter, which makes the lattice SSH-like.

Result. The phonon bands of the metacrystal are topological — their Zak phase jumps by π — defects host edge states, and the frequency comb carries a butterfly-shaped spectral signature of spatio-temporal chirality.

Highlight · Mid-infrared χ⁽²⁾ microcombs, with the University of Freiburg

A CdSiP₂ microresonator pumped by a low-power continuous-wave laser at 1.55 µm generates frequency combs at 3.1 µm through cascaded three-wave mixing — the first mid-infrared microcombs produced this way, spanning about 30 nm.

Result. Besides ordinary combs, the experiment shows staggered combs in which the sidebands around the pump and the half-harmonic alternate; the numerical model developed at Bath reproduces both comb types. Experiment: University of Freiburg and Fraunhofer IPM.
