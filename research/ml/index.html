Physics-consistent machine learning

Two lines of work. The first designs neural operators in which physically meaningful quantities — conserved quantities, moments, spectral statistics — shape the architecture instead of being appended to its input. The second reads physical structure directly from dynamics: Koopman / dynamic mode decomposition to reconstruct band dispersion and quantum geometry, and the identifiability of Hamiltonians from observed trajectories.

Highlight · Multi-Head Residual-Gated DeepONet

The full initial field enters the DeepONet branch as usual, while a compact vector of physical descriptors of that field — peak amplitude, total intensity, centre, width, spectral bandwidth — modulates the operator through three residual pathways and a low-rank multi-head readout.

Result. On the nonlinear Schrödinger benchmark the full-field test error is 1.4 × 10⁻³, against 1.2 × 10⁻² for a vanilla DeepONet and 5.0 × 10⁻³ for FiLM conditioning, at a comparable parameter count. Errors in physical quantities fall with it: the mass error drops from 0.13 to 0.03.

The error maps make the difference visible: the baselines leave structured errors along the soliton trajectories, which MH-RG largely removes. In panels d–g, MH-RG follows the ground-truth physical observables most closely, including the total intensity, which drifts markedly in the vanilla model.
